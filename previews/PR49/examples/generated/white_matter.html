Anisotropic diffusion in white matter ​

In white matter, myelinated axons are packed densely along a common direction. Water diffuses freely along the axons, and is restricted across them. This example simulates a sample of such tissue in a periodic box, with periodic boundary conditions, so that the sample represents a larger piece of tissue.

julia
using SpinDoctor
using LinearAlgebra
using Random
using WGLMakie # Or CairoMakie, GLMakie

Sample ​

Fifteen myelinated axons (radii between 1 and 2.5 µm) fill 65 % of the cross-section (PeriodicBoxECS). The axons are cut by the faces of the box, and their periodic images enter on the opposite faces.

julia
ncell = 15
setup = CylinderSetup(;
    ncell,
    rmin = 1.0,
    rmax = 2.5,
    nsidewall = 16,
    dmin = 0.15,
    layersizes = [0.7, 1.0],
    ecs = PeriodicBoxECS(; fraction = 0.65),
    height = 8.0,
)
mesh, surfaces, cells = create_geometry(setup; rng = Xoshiro(4))
colors = [fill(:steelblue, ncell); fill(:orange, ncell); :lightgray]
plot_mesh(mesh; colors, decorations = false)

The axons, the myelin and the ECS exchange water through permeable membranes. Water diffuses more slowly in the myelin.

julia
coeffs = coefficients(
    setup;
    D = (; cell = [0.002 * I(3), 0.0005 * I(3)], ecs = 0.002 * I(3)),
    T2 = (; cell = [Inf, Inf], ecs = Inf),
    rho = (; cell = [1.0, 1.0], ecs = 1.0),
    kappa = (;
        cell_interfaces = [1e-5],
        cell_boundaries = [0, 0],
        cell_ecs = 1e-5,
        ecs = 0,
    ),
    gamma = 2.67513e-4,
)
model = Model(; mesh, coeffs...);

With periodic = true, the points on opposite faces of the box are identified, and the solvers use periodic boundary conditions instead of impermeable box faces.

julia
matrices = assemble_matrices(model; periodic = true);

Apparent diffusion tensor ​

The homogenized ADC model gives the apparent diffusion tensor of each compartment for all directions at once, here for a PGSE sequence with a diffusion time of 20 ms. The tensor of the sample is the average of the compartments, weighted by their contributions to the signal (compartment_weights).

julia
profile = PGSE(5000.0, 20000.0)
tensors = solve_hadc_tensors(model, matrices, profile)
weights = compartment_weights(model)
tensor = sum(weights .* tensors)
3×3 Matrix{Float64}:
 0.000432254  1.31773e-6   1.38703e-21
 1.31773e-6   0.00041347   2.20453e-22
 1.38703e-21  2.20453e-22  0.00150318

The ADC is largest along the axons (z), with the free diffusion coefficients (the periodic boundary conditions do not restrict the diffusion along the axons), and smaller across:

julia
directions = unitsphere(500)
adcs = [d' * tensor * d for d in eachcol(directions)]
plot_hardi(directions, adcs; label = "ADC [µm²/µs]", decorations = false)

Signal attenuation ​

The Bloch-Torrey equation gives the signal beyond the ADC regime. We compare gradients across (x) and along (z) the axons.

julia
bvalues = [0, 500, 1000, 2000, 3000, 4000]
solver = ThetaSolver(; timestep = 200.0)
signals = map((; across = [1.0, 0.0, 0.0], along = [0.0, 0.0, 1.0])) do dir
    map(bvalues) do b
        gradient = ScalarGradient(dir, profile; bvalue = b, gamma = model.gamma)
        u = solve_btpde(model, matrices, gradient, solver)
        abs(compute_signal(matrices.M, u)) / sum(matrices.volumes)
    end
end
(across = [0.9999999999999957, 0.8248986269581284, 0.7166784886583357, 0.5970215335374133, 0.5313167546170323, 0.4848150658308932], along = [0.9999999999999957, 0.49547200192966684, 0.2724676506829167, 0.10877777600807771, 0.05461494031994304, 0.030226028772183552])

Along the axons, each compartment would attenuate as in free diffusion (dashed line) without exchange. The exchange of water between the slow myelin and the fast compartments makes the slow component decay faster. Across the axons, the signal attenuates slowly, mostly from the ECS, and deviates from a monoexponential decay at higher b-values.

julia
free = [sum(weights .* exp.(-b .* [D[3, 3] for D in model.D])) for b in bvalues]
fig = Figure(; size = (600, 400))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1]; xlabel = "b [s/mm²]", ylabel = "S / S₀", yscale = log10)
scatterlines!(ax, bvalues, signals.across; label = "Across the axons")
scatterlines!(ax, bvalues, signals.along; label = "Along the axons")
lines!(ax, bvalues, free; color = :black, linestyle = :dash, label = "Free, no exchange")
axislegend(ax; position = :lb)
fig

The magnetization across the axons, for b = 4000 s/mm²: the spins dephase in the ECS, while the water in the axons keeps its phase.

julia
gradient = ScalarGradient([1.0, 0.0, 0.0], profile; bvalue = 4000, gamma = model.gamma)
u = solve_btpde(model, matrices, gradient, solver)
plot_field(mesh, u; decorations = false)

The periodic time stepping runs much faster on an NVIDIA GPU, with ThetaSolver(; timestep, arraytype = CuArray) after using CUDA, CUDSS.
